Living Out of Sync

From compulsory sharing to spatial choice on Jurong's industrial waterfront

Location
Jurong, Singapore
Year
2026
  • NUS Design Studio, AY2026/27 Semester 1
  • Exchange, Built Environment
  • Individual project

Migrant workers in Singapore share rooms with people on other shifts. One sleeps while another cooks, washes or calls home, and the only shared space is the room itself.

The project builds a data-driven reading of that friction from 46,759 public reviews of 277 dormitories. Each mention is traced from where it happens in the building, through what residents name, to a problem that can be designed for. Six problems emerge; the most negative is the sanitary core, and the largest are density and territory, and heat, air and sleep.

It then retrofits an existing Jurong dormitory with a suspended gallery of plug-in bays. The gallery does not stand on the ground: outrigger trusses on the existing roof carry steel hanger rods, and each deck hangs from them. Every bedroom opens onto a bay, so cooking, washing and resting can move out of the bedroom, and louvres let each room open or close its own piece of façade.

A kit of plywood cubes furnishes the bays, and the void between the two blocks becomes shared infrastructure for services, stairs and air.
